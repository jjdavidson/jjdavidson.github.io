## The graph and its coloring

The I-graph $I(n,j,k)$ has vertices $u_i,v_i$, with indices taken modulo $n$, and edges

$$
u_i u_{i+j},\qquad v_i v_{i+k},\qquad u_i v_i.
$$

Here $n\geq3$ and $1\leq j,k<n/2$. There are $2n$ vertices and $3n$ edges. The outer rim consists of cycles with step $j$, the inner rim consists of cycles with step $k$, and each spoke joins $u_i$ to $v_i$. Setting $j=1$ recovers the generalized Petersen graph $GP(n,k)$.

Let $d=\gcd(n,j,k)$. The indices in each residue class modulo $d$ form one component, and each component is a copy of $I(n/d,j/d,k/d)$. The chromatic number is

$$
\chi(I(n,j,k))=
\begin{cases}
2,&n/d\text{ even and }j/d,\ k/d\text{ odd},\\
3,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

In a two-coloring, each spoke forces $v_i$ to have the opposite color from $u_i$. Within a component, adding either $j/d$ or $k/d$ to the reduced index must flip the color of the corresponding outer vertex. These steps generate $\mathbb Z/(n/d)\mathbb Z$, so the color flips define a nontrivial homomorphism to $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. This is possible exactly when parity is well defined and both steps are odd. Brooks' theorem gives a three-coloring of each component, which is cubic and has at least six vertices.

## Rotating two cyclic arrangements

For a concentric placement, choose an integer $a$ coprime to $n$ and write

$$
u_i=R e^{2\pi iai/n},\qquad
v_i=r e^{i\theta+2\pi iai/n}.
$$

The choice of $a$ changes the winding while preserving the graph. Use **Step a** to select a winding, or leave it on **Auto** to choose a valid one. Taking

$$
R=\frac{1}{2\lvert\sin(\pi aj/n)\rvert},\qquad
r=\frac{1}{2\lvert\sin(\pi ak/n)\rvert}
$$

makes every outer and inner edge a unit long. All spokes have the same squared length,

$$
\lvert u_i-v_i\rvert^2=R^2+r^2-2Rr\cos\theta.
$$

They become unit edges at

$$
\theta=\arccos\left(\frac{R^2+r^2-1}{2Rr}\right),
$$

provided $\lvert R-r\rvert\leq1\leq R+r$ and the placement has distinct vertices.

## Beyond two concentric circles

Every I-graph has a unit-distance representation. When $j=k$, take two translated copies of the same cyclic arrangement, with the common radius chosen to make each rim edge unit length. The translation gives the spokes. Relative to $u_0u_j$, its direction is $(3+4i)/5$. Ratios of unit chord directions in the arrangement are roots of unity, so this translation keeps the two vertex sets disjoint.

The Nauru graph $I(12,1,5)$ uses a different construction. Let

$$
R=\frac{6+7\sqrt3+\sqrt{15}}{12},\qquad
r=\frac{6+7\sqrt3-\sqrt{15}}{12}.
$$

Place $u_i$ at angle $i\pi/6$, with radius $R$ for even $i$ and $r$ for odd $i$. Place $v_i$ at the same angle and one unit closer to the origin. These four circles realize all of its edges with length one. The graphs $I(12d,d,5d)$ consist of $d$ such components. Exchanging the rims gives $I(12d,5d,d)$.

## From a finite graph to MonCay

The unit edge directions of a completed placement can also be used to generate a MonCay graph, as in [Moser MonCay]({{ '/simulations/moncay/moser-moncay/' | relative_url }}) and [Golomb MonCay]({{ '/simulations/moncay/golomb-moncay/' | relative_url }}). That closure introduces more vertices and edges. A coloring of the displayed finite graph does not by itself provide a coloring of the full MonCay graph.

The visualizer points $u_0\to u_j$ along the unit vector $1$. For a concentric placement, let $\alpha=e^{2\pi ia/n}$ be the rotation for one index advance and set $\sigma=e^{i\theta}$. The first inner rim edge gives $\beta$ and the first spoke gives $\gamma$ in this frame.

$$
\beta=\frac{r\sigma(\alpha^k-1)}{R(\alpha^j-1)},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{r\sigma-R}{R(\alpha^j-1)}.
$$

The directed edge vectors at index $i$ are $\alpha^i$ on the outer rim, $\alpha^i\beta$ on the inner rim, and $\alpha^i\gamma$ on the spokes. The parameters satisfy

$$
\beta=\frac{\alpha^k-1}{\alpha^j-1}+(\alpha^k-1)\gamma.
$$

After translating $u_0$ to the origin, its component lies in $\operatorname{MonCay}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$. Each other component embeds there after translating one of its vertices to the origin.

For translated rims, the first inner rim edge also points along $1$, so $\beta=1$ and the generating set is $\{\alpha,\gamma\}$. For Nauru, $\alpha=e^{i\pi/6}$ rotates one spoke to the next within a component. The even and odd inner rim edges have separate representatives $\beta$ and $\delta$. Odd outer rim directions are signed rotations of $\gamma^2$, so the four generators are $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$.
